About Insider Tracking

A free, independent record of the stock trades U.S. senators report, built from the Senate’s own disclosure site.

{% include "_search.html" %}

How it works

Under the STOCK Act of 2012, senators must report trades of stocks, bonds and other securities worth more than $1,000 within 45 days. The reports are public on the Senate’s Financial Disclosure site, but they are hard to browse there: one report at a time, behind a click-through agreement.

Every three hours, a small program reads the Senate site, saves new reports in a database, and rebuilds this website from it. Nothing is typed in by hand, and nothing is left out on purpose: if it is in an electronic report, it is here.

Insider Tracking is non-commercial and carries no advertising. It is not affiliated with the U.S. Senate or any senator.

Data and methods

  • Source. U.S. Senate Financial Disclosures, efdsearch.senate.gov, Periodic Transaction Reports filed since 2012. Every trade links to its report there.
  • Amounts. Senators report value ranges, such as $15,001–$50,000, not exact amounts or numbers of shares. The site shows the ranges as filed. Filters on amount use the lower end of the range.
  • Days to disclose. For a trade, the number of days between the trade and the day the Senate received the report. The law allows 45 days; trades past that are marked with a clock.
  • Late reports. Late filing is counted per report, not per trade, so one large report doesn’t weigh more than a small one: a report is late when the oldest trade in it is more than 45 days old on the day it is filed.
  • Amendments. A senator can correct an earlier report, and the amendment usually lists every trade again. Those rows are shown and labelled, but they are left out of totals, charts and the late-filing counts, so a corrected trade isn’t counted twice.
  • Paper filings. Some reports are filed on paper and scanned. They are counted as reports but their trades can’t be read automatically yet, so they aren’t in the trade lists.
  • Who is who. The Senate site gives names only. Party and state come from the public-domain congress-legislators dataset. Candidates who never served aren’t in that dataset, so they appear without party or state.
  • What is not here. Trades by members of the House of Representatives, trades under $1,000, and holdings from yearly financial reports.

Contact and corrections

Found a mistake, or a report that isn’t showing? Open an issue in the project’s repository on GitHub: github.com/{{ repo }}/issues. The code that collects the data and builds this site is public there too.

Legal

Federal law (5 U.S.C. § 13107) forbids using financial disclosure reports for any commercial purpose other than news reporting, for credit ratings, or for soliciting money. This site is for public information only.
